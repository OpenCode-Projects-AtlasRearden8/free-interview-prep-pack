Day-Of Timeline

Pencil in your own times, then follow the page instead of the panic. When each step has a home, nothing lives in your head rent-free.

When What Done
Night before Outfit out (whole thing), bag packed by the door, paper copies printed. Phone alarm set — two, five minutes apart.  
Night before Skim this pack once with the light you'd actually use. Then a real wind-down — sleep is a better study aid than midnight flashcards.  
_____ Morning: eat something, drink water. Practice one question card out loud while getting ready — that's the whole warm-up.  
_____ Leave 15 minutes early, headed for the building. Today is allowed to need a buffer.  
_____ Arrive nearby, not inside: a café, your car, around the corner. Pocket the phone. Breathe slow, four counts in, six out, three times.  
_____ Walk in 10–15 minutes early. Check in, greet the first person by name if you catch it. Restroom mirror check — one glance, done.  
During Ask your questions. Caught by a surprise question? "Let me think for a second" is a fine answer — thinking out loud counts too. Names go on the notepad.  
Leaving "Thank you — spending the time with you was worth it." Get a name and email for each person you met, so the thank-you finds everyone.  
Same day Write the thank-you (email version while it's fresh). Log names and any newly promised next steps.  
Tonight Fill the Post-Interview Reflection Log ONCE, kindly. Then take the actual evening off. You earned whole hours, not just permission to rest "later."  
If timing collapses anyway (bad bus, wrong building), the fix is one call: a steady "running about ten minutes behind, my apologies, I'm on my way." People reschedule around honesty far more often than panic predicts.

Leave the done column half empty? That's what the sheet is for — the blanks tell you tomorrow's adjustments. Checklists aren't verdicts; they're memory insurance.
